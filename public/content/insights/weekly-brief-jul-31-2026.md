## 12. Weekly Insight

> "There is no soft inflation target. There's only a target, and it's 2%." — Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, July 29 press conference

This was a week priced by an oxymoron: the hawkish hold. Rates did not move, but the message moved toward hikes — three dissents for a hike and Warsh's rhetoric translated instantly into a 5.21% 30-year yield, the highest since 2007. What deserves attention is crypto's reaction function. BTC lost $64K briefly after the FOMC, but the drawdown stopped near -3% and the week closed +1.65%. That kind of downside rigidity during a long-end yield spike — arriving just as US public debt crossed 100% of GDP — suggests the "fiscal-risk hedge" narrative BTC shares with gold is beginning to put a floor under price. Gold flirting with $4,100 while BTC firmed is not a coincidence.

The quality of capital inflow, however, is weak. July's ~+$205M into spot BTC ETFs is the smallest month on record, YTD flows remain -$4.8B, and even Strategy has now gone five weeks without buying — indeed it sold 3,588 BTC in July. Both marquee bid sources of this cycle (ETFs and corporate treasuries) are simultaneously on pause. The reason price holds anyway is on-chain: exchange BTC balances are at 2017-era lows with -4,624 BTC leaving in a week, so the sellable float itself keeps thinning, and MVRV at 1.22 (Z-score 0.38) is nowhere near overheated. It is a standoff between absent demand and shrinking supply — one that could tip toward demand the moment the 8/7 jobs print or 8/12 CPI revives rate-cut expectations.

Structurally, a qualitative rotation of capital is underway. Within stablecoins, money moved from USDe (-9.5%) to BUIDL (+21.0%) — from synthetic carry to tokenized Treasuries — while protocol revenue remains dominated by Tether and Circle. The longer rates stay high, the more crypto's earnings center on "businesses that carry interest rates on-chain," which supports reading the RWA sector ($34.7B) and the Korean/European institutionalization push as cash-flow-backed growth rather than a theme. Conversely, Hyperliquid's SK Hynix incident warned that the race to tokenize equities is outrunning oracle-design basics, and CME's lawsuit against the CFTC shows incumbent exchanges now treat on-chain derivatives as an existential threat.

Tactically, next week is event-dense. The 8/5 QRA will steer the long end (30Y at 5.2%), the 8/7 NFP will set September FOMC expectations, and the same day marks the effective deadline for the CLARITY Act's regulatory premium. If all three break favorably, the base path is a $65,150 breakout toward $66–68K; if not, a retest of $62.5K support.

**Asset allocation view (qualitative; not investment advice):** Maintain core BTC/ETH on shrinking on-chain supply and undemanding valuations; evidence is accumulating for a modest ETH tilt within core (ETF relative strength, TVL rebound). Keep satellites confined to assets with verified fees/revenue (perps infrastructure, RWA issuance chains, revenue-generating DePIN); reduce low-float alts with heavy unlock overhangs and equity-perp exposure with unproven oracle designs. Keep leverage conservative into the 8/5–8/7 event window.
