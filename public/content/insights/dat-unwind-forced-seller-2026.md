## 1. The Event and the Numbers

On July 6, Michael Saylor announced that Strategy had sold 3,588 BTC for $216M to fund dividends on its Digital Credit securities, leaving 843,775 BTC and $2.55B in USD reserves as of July 5.

Unpacking the numbers:

- Per the 8-K, two tranches: 1,363 BTC at an average $59,256 (June 29–30) plus 2,225 BTC at an average $60,773 (July 1–5). Blended implied price: roughly **$60,200**. Against the cost basis of **$75,476**, that's ~$15K per coin — about **$54M below cost**.
- This is the third sale in company history. The first was December 2022 (704 BTC, tax-loss harvesting); the second was this May 26–31 — 32 BTC for $2.5M at an average $77,135, symbolically the first sale in three and a half years but negligible in size. Five weeks later, the sale is **112x larger by coin count** and the **largest ever** by both coins and dollars.
- And yet the market's response was not collapse. MSTR rebounded sharply from its late-June low (~$82) to **reclaim the $100 level** (July 2 close: $100.77; all prices as of July 6 unless noted). What that rebound means comes later.

## 2. Why This Didn't Cause the Drawdown

The "Strategy sold, so the price fell" reading doesn't survive the arithmetic.

US spot BTC ETFs posted **$4.5B in net outflows** in June (per SoSoValue/Farside) — the worst month since the products launched in January 2024, exceeding the prior record ($3.48B, February 2025) by roughly 29%. BlackRock's IBIT alone accounted for $3.55B, or 79%. BTC fell 20.48% in June — its worst month since June 2022 — and hit a YTD low of $58,190. The triggers were macro: the Warsh Fed's hawkish dot-plot shift erased rate-cut expectations, and the SpaceX IPO on June 12 absorbed risk capital.

Against that backdrop, Strategy's $216M is **1/20th** of the ETF outflows. By volume it's smaller still — executed over five trading days, roughly $43M per day, well under 1% of daily BTC spot volume depending on how you measure it. This did not cause the drop. The causality runs the other way: **liquidity dried up, and the structure that must sell into weakness started to run.**

Strategy's real market impact isn't the coins it sold — it's the bid that disappeared. The company raised $25.3B in 2025 alone — the largest equity issuer among US public companies for a second consecutive year — to buy BTC as the cycle's biggest marginal buyer. That buyer is now a seller taking losses. Recall May's 32 BTC — 0.004% of holdings — where BTC slid about 2% on the day of the announcement. That move admittedly landed on an already-weak macro tape, but the amplifier clearly wasn't the volume; it was the signal that "Strategy sells." When the marginal buyer becomes the marginal seller, price reacts not to the news but to the transition itself.

## 3. The Structural Flaw — Debt Premised on Defending a Price

The core of the problem is STRC (Stretch): Strategy's flagship Digital Credit security, a variable-rate perpetual preferred designed to hold near its $100 stated amount. At roughly $10.5–11B face, it dominates the ~$15.5B preferred stack.

The design logic: if the price slips below $100, raise the dividend rate to create buying incentive and restore par. Elegant — with one prerequisite. **The company must be able to keep paying that raised dividend.**

Which raises the fundamental question. Nobody can predict market volatility. Price can diverge from fundamentals further, and for longer, than any model allows. Yet the capital structure was built on a zero-yield asset (BTC) and premised on defending a specific security's market price. BTC generates no staking rewards, no interest, no cash flow. The legacy software business brings in roughly $0.48B in annual revenue (2025: $477M) — with free cash flow well below that — nowhere near the ~$1.76B in annual dividend and interest obligations. That leaves exactly two funding sources: **issuance or sales.** When issuance closes, one remains.

The Digital Credit Capital Framework announced June 29 institutionalized this. The company must maintain at least 12 months of obligation coverage at all times (currently $2.55B = 17.4 months); the board authorized up to $1.25B of BTC sales for reserve replenishment; and $1B common plus $1B preferred repurchase programs run alongside. Sales are not mandated — but they are now a standing, formalized option. "Never sell" is over, in writing.

## 4. The Three Gears

If this structure fails, it won't fall off a single cliff. It will grind down through three interlocking gears.

**Gear 1 — The dividend ratchet.** STRC's variable rate exists to defend $100, and so far it has moved in only one direction: launched at 9% in August 2025, raised repeatedly to 11.25% in February, 11.5% in March, and 12% effective July 1. Even so, STRC fell to a record low of $71.25 on June 26 and has recovered only to the $88–90 range post-framework (as of July 6) — below par despite a 12% coupon, implying a yield near 13%. The market is demanding trust, not coupon. The more you defend, the heavier the burden; the heavier the burden, the harder the defense. The ratchet has never yet shown it can turn the other way.

**Gear 2 — Burn-rate acceleration.** The issue here isn't the physical volume of the sales — it's the "forced seller" signal that repeated sales create. Obligations are fixed in dollars ($1.76B/yr); assets are denominated in BTC. Under the worst-case assumption that issuance is fully closed and dividends are funded purely by sales (note: in reality, even in June the company raised ~$1.15B via ATM):

| BTC price | BTC required per year | % of holdings |
|---|---|---|
| $80K | ~22,000 BTC | 2.6% |
| $60K | ~29,300 BTC | 3.5% |
| $50K | ~35,200 BTC | 4.2% |
| $40K | ~44,000 BTC | 5.2% |

Halve the price, and the coins burned for the same dividend double. And those sales — as signal, not volume — press the price again. That is the arithmetic of the reversed flywheel. The original machine was "issue at a premium → buy BTC → BTC per share rises → premium holds," and every step required the premium. At a discount, the same machine runs in reverse.

**Gear 3 — The maturity wall.** The real cliff is a date, not a price. Convertible notes outstanding total roughly $6.7B (after May's repurchase of $1.5B of the 2029 notes at an 8% discount to face). With conversion prices of $149.77–$672.40 deeply out of the money at current stock levels, these are cash obligations, not equity, unless the stock recovers. The first investor put on the remaining converts is September 15, 2027 (the 2028 notes), with further puts and maturities through 2028. If mNAV recovers by then, the wall becomes a door — refinancing. If not, the wall is a wall.

## 5. Why "$40K Margin Call" Is a Myth

Steelman the doomers' numbers. The most widely circulated scenario on social media: "If BTC breaks $40K, Strategy gets margin-called and 840,000 coins hit the market." Open the balance sheet and the scenario fails at step one.

**First, there is no collateral to call.** The $6.7B of convertible notes are entirely **unsecured** senior debt; not a single BTC is pledged. Coupons run 0–0.625% — effectively zero. With no collateral, there is no legal mechanism by which creditors can force early repayment or seize coins because the price fell — at $40K or $20K. The only loan that ever carried a liquidation clause — the $205M Silvergate facility — was repaid early, at roughly a 22% discount, back in **March 2023**.

**Second, asset coverage is ample.** At $40K BTC, holdings are worth 843,775 × $40K = **$33.8B**, against ~$22.2B of claims senior to common (the $6.7B converts plus ~$15.5B of preferred at face) — roughly **1.5x**. Note that the preferreds are perpetual securities with no maturity and no redemption right: there is no "day the bill comes due." On debt alone, assets don't equal liabilities until BTC reaches roughly $8K.

**Third, there's a time buffer.** The $2.55B USD reserve covers 17.4 months of obligations with no further sales, and ~26 months including the $1.25B sale cap. And the cap itself is limited to 2.5% of holdings — by board policy, a "dump everything" fire sale is structurally impossible to execute.

So what actually breaks at lower prices isn't liquidation — it's other things: the 12-month coverage floor tightens, forced-sale volumes grow (Gear 2), the mNAV discount deepens and closes the issuance window, and fair-value accounting damage (Q1 net loss of $12.5B on a $14.5B mark-to-market hit) plus the S&P B- rating (assigned October 2025) compound. Not death — **transformation.** Strategy has quietly become a credit vehicle that must sell its assets into weakness to fund dividends. As credit analyst Austin Campbell (founder of Zero Knowledge, formerly Paxos) put it: "This isn't a death spiral — it could just melt for five years." The point is not "it dies." The point is that if it dies, it dies in this order — ratchet → burn rate → maturity wall — and by melting, not exploding.

## 6. How to Read the Rebound — mNAV 1.22x

The company sold its core asset at a loss and raised its dividend to 12% — and MSTR reclaimed $100 instead of collapsing. It looks paradoxical until you run the mNAV arithmetic.

The benchmark came straight from management. On the Q1 earnings call (May 5), CEO Phong Le said: **"Right now it's about 1.22x. Above 1.22x mNAV, it's more accretive for us to issue equity and buy Bitcoin; below 1.22x, it's actually more accretive to sell Bitcoin and pay off our dividends."** In other words, 1.22x isn't an analyst estimate — it's the company's own stated breakeven. And mNAV has now compressed to around 1.0x. Within that frame, this BTC sale plus the $2B repurchase program is capital allocation executed exactly by the manual.

One crucial distinction here. Within management's framework, selling BTC is **locally rational** arbitrage. But the structural flaw isn't the act of selling — it's **the fact that mNAV was forced below 1.22x, killing the original flywheel of "buy BTC with equity premium."** The flaw is that a rational response became necessary at all.

Market reactions split accordingly. Crypto natives fixated on the "never sell betrayed" narrative. On the institutional side, Benchmark (Buy, $570 target maintained) and Bernstein ("forced selling unlikely") read it positively. For balance: TD Cowen kept its Buy but cut its target from $400 to $260, and Citi ($136), Canaccord ($130), BTIG ($250), and Mizuho ($213) all lowered targets over the same stretch. Framework positive, BTC outlook lowered — that is the precise shape of the institutional consensus.

And the verdict is still out. **The common-stock rebound is not the verdict; the verdict comes from credit.** As long as STRC cannot return to $100 par despite a 12% coupon, the ratchet and the sale pressure remain. A preferred trading at a double-digit discount to par means the market is still pricing credit risk. Whether the bullish read (orderly small sales cut the tail risk) or the bearish read (the structure is unchanged; they only bought time) proves right doesn't need to be decided today — that's what pre-written verdict criteria are for.

## 7. Not All DATs Are Built Alike

This stress is a function of structure, not of the asset class. Compare:

**BitMine (BMNR)** also issued a 9.5% Series A perpetual preferred (BMNP) in June. Three differences. ① Scale — ~$274M raised, ~$33M/yr in dividend burden vs Strategy's $1.76B. ② The collateral is productive — staked ETH generates $235–277M/yr in rewards (company estimates), covering the dividend ~7x with native yield. No ETH needs to be sold. ③ No price peg, no variable rate — BMNP is fixed at 9.5%. The caveat: staking rewards are ETH-denominated, so a falling ETH price shrinks their dollar value. The reflexivity isn't zero — just weak.

The dividing line is clear: **leveraged structures with an embedded forced-sale mechanism vs those without one.** A DAT built on pure common-equity ATM simply stops buying in a bear market. A leveraged DAT that promised to defend a price must sell into one. The tide going out is revealing exactly this difference — who was wearing trunks.

## 8. Whose Problem Is This — Two Layers of Contagion

**Layer one — the holders.** Per Strategy's own investor materials (May 2026), MSTR is the most widely held BTC proxy in the world, spread across roughly 1,400 institutions, ETFs and funds, and hundreds of thousands of retail accounts. Disclosed institutional holders include major pensions like CalPERS and CalSTRS (13F-confirmed), and holdings by overseas pensions and insurers — including Korea's National Pension Service (NPS) — have been repeatedly reported. Index inclusion (Nasdaq 100 since December 2024, among others) exposes passive money automatically. The STRC-style preferreds are believed to be held heavily by retail income investors drawn to double-digit yields.

**Layer two — the imitators.** The wider contagion path runs here. More than 190 listed companies worldwide have copied MSTR's treasury model (Metaplanet with 43,000+ BTC, MARA with 36,000+, among others). The problem: most are small and mid caps with neither Strategy's $2.55B cash wall nor its perpetual-preferred funding machine. Now that the guardian of "never sell" has formally institutionalized a sale program, liquidity-strapped imitators have a precedent that selling is permitted. This is exactly where JPMorgan's warning lands — the framework introduced **two-way risk** into the market. Where "MSTR will buy the dip" once supported the downside psychologically, "MSTR might sell the dip" now enters the pricing.

And at the market-structure level: Strategy holds roughly 4% of BTC's 21-million total supply cap. A holder of that scale sitting in a structural-seller state is now a standing variable in BTC market microstructure.

## 9. The Verdict Framework

**The autopsy sequence (if it fails, in this order):**
① STRC fails to reclaim par despite dividend hikes; the ratchet climbs further → ② Sale run-rate rises, burning through the $1.25B cap; the cap gets raised → ③ The mNAV discount entrenches, keeping the issuance window shut → ④ Refinancing pressure at the 2027–2028 convert puts and maturities. The endpoint is not bankruptcy — it is the multi-year melt: issuance closed, forced sales expanding, holdings and common-equity value grinding down. Every step is observable.

**The recovery sequence (if it survives, in this order):**
① A liquidity regime shift (Fed pivot) lifts BTC → ② STRC returns to par; the ratchet turns the other way for the first time (a dividend cut) → ③ mNAV crosses back above 1.22x, issuance resumes, the flywheel spins forward again → ④ Sales stop; the repurchase programs retire discounted securities — and the episode gets re-narrated as "temporary stress."

**Three monitoring indicators:**
1. **STRC price vs $100 par** — the true thermometer of this structure. A break back below $95 signals the ratchet re-arming
2. **mNAV vs 1.22x** — the direction of the flywheel. Above 1.22x = issuance resumes; stuck below 1.0x = deepening sale dependence
3. **Monthly BTC sale run-rate vs the $1.25B cap** — the speed of buffer depletion

A month ago we asked: does DAT become the trigger for this cycle? Today's answer: "Not MSTR alone — but through 190 imitators, it can be." When leverage unwinds, symptoms become triggers. Exactly as our Q2 report concluded — this asset class is a function of liquidity, not news, and liquidity is reading out its verdict right now. We wrote down the grading criteria before the verdict came in.
