## 1. Executive Summary

### Headline theme: "The Warsh Fed and the Great Divergence — Record-High Equities, Capitulating Crypto"

Q2 2026 will be remembered as a quarter of extreme decoupling within risk assets. Powered by the end of the US–Iran war (the Islamabad Memorandum signed June 17)²⁰ and the AI rally, the S&P 500 surged **+14.1% to +18.2% for the quarter** (range reflects vendor calculation bases; the largest quarterly gain since Q2 2020) to fresh all-time highs,¹¹ and Korea's KOSPI delivered roughly +70% for the quarter and **+101.1% for the first half** — the best half-year in its history.¹⁹ Meanwhile, Bitcoin fell **-20% in June alone** (its worst month since June 2022) and on June 24 broke below **$60,000 for the first time since 2024**.²⁵ ²⁶ BTC ended the quarter at roughly **$58,500–$59,900** (source-dependent), down **about -13% QoQ** and **about -45% YoY**,²⁷ ²⁸ while Ethereum finished at roughly **$1,560–$1,610**, down **about -25% QoQ** — its **first-ever three-consecutive-quarter losing streak**.²⁹

The cause of the divergence was unambiguously macro. The Iran-war energy shock reignited inflation, with US May CPI hitting **+4.2% YoY**, the highest since April 2023,⁴ and the June FOMC — the first under **new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh**, sworn in May 22 — lifted the 2026 year-end dot-plot median from 3.4% to **3.8%** (9 of 18 members projecting at least one hike this year), extinguishing easing expectations.² ³ The ECB delivered its first hike in three years on June 11 (deposit rate 2.25%),⁷ and the BOJ raised its policy rate to **1.00%** on June 16, the highest since 1995.⁸ Crypto, the asset class most sensitive to liquidity-driven valuation, absorbed this tightening reversal head-on.

Institutional flows reversed violently. US spot Bitcoin ETFs swung from **+$2.44B of net inflows in April** (the best month since October 2025) to **-$4.06B in June** — the largest monthly outflow since launch (vendor range -$4.06B to -$4.5B) — and closed the quarter on a **seven-week outflow streak totaling more than -$7.7B**.⁴¹ ⁴² ⁴³ Sentiment was further crushed by Strategy's (formerly MicroStrategy) **first BTC sale since 2022** (32 BTC in late May, followed by a June 29 authorization to sell up to $1.25B) and Mt. Gox's movement of 10,422 BTC.⁵¹ ⁵⁵ In contrast, the rotation into **yield-bearing (staking) altcoin ETFs** continued: XRP ETFs logged eight consecutive weeks of inflows, with cumulative inflows of roughly $1.47B.⁴⁶

On-chain and structural indicators pointed to capitulation. The MVRV Z-Score reached **0.20** and NUPL **0.12**, historic bottom territory,³⁶ and the Fear & Greed Index closed above 50 on only a single day during the entire quarter.²⁹ Mining difficulty was cut **-10.09% on June 15** (the 11th-largest downward adjustment ever), with the network's average production cost (\~$87,000) far above market price — a textbook miner capitulation.³⁸ On the security front, the quarter set an all-time record with **83 incidents and roughly $755M–$775M in losses**; North Korea's Lazarus Group accounted for 76% of the year's stolen value with just two attacks — Drift ($285M) and KelpDAO ($292M).⁹⁶ ⁹⁸ In Korea, in stark contrast to the KOSPI boom, KRW exchange volumes collapsed to a 30-month low and the negative kimchi premium persisted, evidencing a retail exodus from crypto.³⁹ ⁴⁰

**Key quarterly themes (6)**

- **Great Divergence**: S&P 500 +14–18% at record highs vs BTC -13% (June -20%) — the equity–crypto correlation broke down¹¹ ²⁵

### 2.2 The Federal Reserve — Powell Exits, Warsh Arrives, the Dots Turn Hawkish

The Fed held the policy rate at **3.50–3.75%** throughout the quarter, but beneath the surface a historic transition in US monetary policy played out.

**April 28–29 FOMC**: rates were held, but the vote split **8–4** — the most dissents since October 1992. Governor Stephen Miran argued for a 25bp cut, while Hammack, Kashkari, and Logan objected to the statement's easing bias. The minutes noted that war-driven energy prices were feeding inflation and that policy might need to stay on hold "for longer than previously anticipated."¹

**The chair transition**: Chair Powell's term expired May 15. **Kevin Warsh** was confirmed by the Senate on May 13 (54–45 — the narrowest confirmation of a Fed chair in history) and **sworn in on May 22**. Powell remains on the Board of Governors.²

**June 16–17 FOMC (Warsh's first as Chair)**: a unanimous hold, but hawkish in substance. The 2026 year-end dot-plot median was raised from 3.4% to **3.8%**; of 18 participants, **9 projected at least one hike this year**, 8 no change, and only 1 a cut. The statement was drastically shortened, easing-bias language was removed, and Warsh announced task forces to overhaul Fed operations.³ Market expectations of a 2026 cut effectively vanished; the debate shifted to whether the next move is a hike.

**Inflation and employment**: US CPI accelerated all quarter — March **+3.3%** → April **+3.8%** → May **+4.2%** (the highest since April 2023 and the first 4-handle in three years). May energy prices rose +23.5% YoY and drove the spike, while core CPI at +2.9% remained comparatively stable, marking this as a geopolitics-driven supply shock.⁴ ⁵ Employment stayed firm: April nonfarm payrolls **+115K** (vs +55K expected), May **+172K** (vs +80K expected), with unemployment at **4.3%** both months.⁶ On a YoY basis, CPI has risen from +2.4% (May 2025) to +4.2% while the policy rate has fallen from 4.25–4.50% to 3.50–3.75%,²² dramatically raising the Fed's policy discomfort.

### 2.3 The ECB — First Hike in Three Years

On April 30 the ECB held the deposit rate at 2.00%, delivering a stagflationary diagnosis: "upside risks to inflation and downside risks to growth have intensified."⁷ But with euro-area May inflation at 3.2%, the ECB **raised the deposit rate from 2.00% to 2.25% on June 11** — the first hike since 2023. New staff projections put 2026 HICP at 3.0% and 2027 at 2.3%, showing how thoroughly the Iran-war energy shock rewrote the euro-area inflation path.⁷

### 2.4 The BOJ — Highest Policy Rate Since 1995

The BOJ held at 0.75% on April 28, but the 6–3 vote (Nakagawa, Takata, and Tamura dissenting in favor of 1.0%) revealed building hawkish pressure, and the FY2026 core CPI forecast was lifted sharply from 1.9% to 2.8%.⁸ On **June 16 the policy rate was raised from 0.75% to 1.00%** (7–1) — the **highest in roughly 30 years, since 1995**. The yen attempted to strengthen from around 160 per dollar after the decision. With two of the world's three major central banks (ECB, BOJ) hiking in the same month, the signal of renewed global tightening was unmistakable.⁸

### 2.5 The Bank of Korea — On Hold, but Hike Dissents Emerge

The BOK held its base rate at **2.50%** unanimously on April 10 (a seventh consecutive hold),⁹ and held again on May 28 — but with **two members (Chang Yong-sung, Ryoo Sang-dae) dissenting in favor of a hike**. May minutes showed even hold voters flagging the need to consider future hikes, raising the odds that the BOK's next move is up, not down. The BOK also raised its 2026 growth forecast from 2.0% to **2.6%**.⁹

Korean consumer inflation accelerated: April +2.6% → May **+3.1%** → June **+3.2%** (the largest rise in 30 months). June petroleum products surged **+24.7%** (diesel +33.7%); absent the government's fuel price cap, the print would have been \~3.6%.²¹ The won firmed to 1,472.7 on April 8 during the ceasefire window, then weakened anew — closing above 1,540 for the first time in 17 years on June 24 and ending the quarter at 1,548–1,555 (vendor range).¹⁵

### 2.6 Geopolitics: The End of the Iran War and the "Post-War Rally"

The Iran war, which began February 28, moved to resolution during the quarter. A Pakistan-brokered two-week ceasefire took effect **April 8**, but Iran refused to reopen the Strait of Hormuz; after the Islamabad talks collapsed, the US declared a **naval blockade of Iran on April 13**, re-escalating tensions.²⁰ Following repeated ceasefire extensions through late May, a framework agreement was announced **June 15**, and on **June 17** President Trump and Iranian President Pezeshkian signed the **Islamabad Memorandum**, formally ending the war. Terms included lifting Hormuz restrictions, ending the naval blockade, US force reductions, sanctions relief, and a 60-day timeline for nuclear talks.²⁰

Oil traced this arc precisely. After the March 9 intraday war peak of $119.47, WTI re-touched \~$115 in early/mid-April before collapsing on peace expectations to finish June 30 at **about $70** (June -20%+, the worst month since 2021).¹⁶ Gold posted a double-digit quarterly decline as safe-haven demand evaporated (about -10% to -14% on a spot basis; -16% per CNBC's calculation — the worst quarter since 2013).¹⁷ The post-war risk revival flowed exclusively into equities. The S&P 500 reclaimed 7,000 on April 15, erasing all war losses, and closed above 7,600 for the first time ever on June 2.¹² The KOSPI, led by semiconductors (Samsung Electronics +178.6% YTD, SK hynix +307.1% YTD), printed its first-ever 9,000 close (9,063.84) on June 18; its quarterly gain reached roughly +70%.¹⁹ Yet the simultaneous global tightening squeezed crypto and growth valuations — **the breakdown of the equity–crypto correlation** is this quarter's defining macro conclusion.

### 4.2 Bitcoin (BTC) Deep Dive

#### 4.2.1 Price trajectory: "April relief rally → May top → June capitulation"

**Phase 1 — April: relief rally (+11.9%)**. Boosted by the Iran ceasefire (4/8) and the ETF flow reversal (+$2.44B), BTC gained **+11.87%** in April, closing the month near $76,580²³ ⁴¹ — the first meaningful bounce after Q1's disaster (-22–23%, the worst opening quarter since 2018).³⁰

**Phase 2 — May: top and reversal**. In early May BTC broke **above $81,000**, its highest since January (up to $82,849 per some sources).²⁴ The April CPI shock (+3.8%, released May 12) and Chair Warsh's arrival turned the tide; ETFs flipped to -$2.41B of May outflows, and news of Strategy's first-ever sale landed late in the month. BTC closed May near $74,000.⁴² ⁵¹

**Phase 3 — June: the -20% crash and the $60K break**. June was the worst month since June 2022 (-20%).²⁵ The June 10 CPI shock (+4.2%) and the June 17 hawkish dot plot erased liquidity hopes, and $4.06B left the ETFs during the month. On June 24 BTC surrendered $60,000 for the first time since 2024 (a 21-month low), slid to an intraday $58,100 on June 25,²⁶ and roughly $1B of liquidations ($780M longs) hit on June 29–30, with sub-$58,000 prints on some venues on the final day.²⁷ The June 30 close of roughly $58,500–$59,900 stands about **-50% below the October 2025 all-time high of $126,000**.²⁷ The Fear & Greed Index closed the quarter at 18 (Extreme Fear), having exceeded 50 on only one day all quarter.²⁹ ¹⁰³

#### 4.2.2 On-chain: historic bottom territory

**Valuation**: as of July 1, the **MVRV Z-Score stood at 0.20 and NUPL at 0.12**, with market cap converging on realized cap.³⁶ Historically, MVRV Z-Scores at or below 0.2 have been observed only near cycle bottoms. Caution is warranted, however: in the ETF era the realized price has been structurally lifted, compressing this indicator's signal (even the 2025 cycle top registered only +1.2–2.5). Absolute thresholds should give way to interpretation alongside NUPL, realized price, and other complements — all of which are lagging.

**Holder behavior**: per Glassnode, long-term holders flipped from months of distribution to **net accumulation of roughly 50,000–100,000 BTC**, strongest in the sub-1 BTC and 100–1,000 BTC cohorts (accumulation trend scores of 0.8–0.9) — moving in the exact opposite direction of record ETF outflows.³⁷ Exchange BTC balances vary widely by provider (1.8M–3M BTC) but agree directionally on multi-year lows.³⁷

#### 4.2.3 Mining economics: capitulation

Hashrate fell from the October 2025 all-time high of **1.1 ZH/s (1,100 EH/s)** to **918 EH/s** around the June adjustment and **886 EH/s** by late June (June -12%; -23% from peak).³⁸ Difficulty was adjusted **-10.09% on June 15** (138.96T → 124.93T) — the 11th-largest downward adjustment ever and the second-biggest of 2026. Hashprice rebounded +13% post-adjustment to \~$33/PH/s/day, barely reclaiming gross breakeven (\~$30).³⁸ With the network's average production cost near **$87,000** versus a $58–60K market price, higher-cost rigs powered down en masse. The listed miners' AI/HPC pivot accelerated: Bitfarms dropped "Bitcoin" from its name, and activists are pushing Riot toward AI data centers.³⁸

### 4.3 Ethereum (ETH) Deep Dive

#### 4.3.1 Price: first-ever three-quarter losing streak

ETH fell from $2,133.44 at the start of the quarter to roughly $1,560–$1,610 on June 30, **about -25%**, again underperforming BTC (-13%) badly.²³ ²⁷ ²⁹ The June monthly low was near $1,505. This marks ETH's **first three consecutive negative quarters ever**, leaving it about -68% below the August 2025 all-time high (\~$4,950).²⁹ The ETH/BTC ratio sat near multi-year lows around **0.0268** at quarter-end.

#### 4.3.2 The Ethereum Foundation restructuring — the quarter's biggest ecosystem shock

On June 23 the Ethereum Foundation announced a sweeping restructuring: **54 positions cut (\~20% of \~270 staff) and a 40% reduction in the 2026 operating budget**. The organization was reorganized into five clusters (protocol, access, user, community, institutional), with a shift to an endowment model targeting a cut in spending from \~15% to \~5% of treasury by 2030. Nine senior figures — including both co-executive directors — have departed since January 2026, and former EF researchers launched **Ethlabs**, backed by more than $11B in ETH.³¹ The market read this less as cost discipline than as a leadership vacuum and roadmap uncertainty, further damaging the valuation narrative.

#### 4.3.3 Staking and supply dynamics

- **Staking**: as of June 15, **39.67M ETH** (31.98% of supply) was staked across **1.24M validators**, up 4.05M ETH YTD.³² The entry queue held 3.59M ETH (62-day wait) while the exit queue stood at zero — staking demand stayed firm despite price weakness.
- **Yield**: native APR compressed to **2.78%** (+0.5–1.0pp MEV).³²
- **Supply**: with gas averaging \~0.15 gwei in early June, the burn collapsed and ETH remained net inflationary. In one June week, 94,525 ETH was issued against only 324 ETH burned — **+0.83% annualized inflation**.³² ³³ The **"L2 expansion paradox"** — record usage alongside collapsing fees and burn — dominated the ETH valuation debate all quarter, inflamed by Vitalik Buterin's remark that the current L2 model "no longer makes sense."³³ ⁶³

### 4.4 Altcoins and Market Structure

**Dominance and breadth**: BTC dominance held higher at **\~55.5–56%** at quarter-end (H1 range 54–59%), with the Altcoin Season Index at 43–49 — still "Bitcoin season."³⁴ ³⁵ June breadth was brutal: **82.1% of the top 100 declined**, the median return was -16.8%, and large caps such as ADA (-38.2%) and BCH (-35.2%) were hit hard. All eight tracked narrative sectors were negative (L2 -24.9%, DePIN -24.8%, L1 -22.8%).²⁹

**Relative strength — XRP and SOL**: assets with staking-yield spot ETFs showed defensive resilience. XRP ETFs recorded eight straight weeks of inflows (cumulative \~$1.47B) and SOL ETFs $1.12B cumulative; SOL held up comparatively well even during the June 30 break below $60K.⁴⁶ ⁴⁷ The "BTC/ETH ETFs → XRP/SOL yield ETFs" rotation is judged the clearest institutional flow path of mid-2026.⁴⁶

**Korea — kimchi premium and the volume collapse**: the **negative premium** confirmed in 2026 (Upbit BTC around -0.9%, March reading) persisted (exact June prints: data unverified).³⁹ The more structural signal was volume: April's combined KRW-exchange turnover fell to **$55.09B, a 30-month low** (Upbit $34.36B, Bithumb $15.51B), and Upbit's global exchange ranking slid from \~4th to the **26th–30th range**.⁴⁰ Domestic retail capital rotated into the KOSPI (+101% H1), with looming crypto gains taxation adding pressure. The top ticker by turnover on Upbit was not BTC but **XRP**.⁴⁰

### 12.2 Macro Outlook: Hike or Re-Easing?

### 12.4 Outlook

Q2 2026 was, in a word, the quarter of **"liquidity's verdict."** That crypto failed to rally even on the ultimate good news — the end of the war — reconfirms that this asset class remains a function of global real rates and dollar liquidity. Against the "tightening reversal" of the Warsh Fed's hawkish turn and simultaneous ECB/BOJ hikes, the peace rally flowed only into equities, while crypto was left to digest a triple burden of ETF redemptions, treasury selling, and hacks alone.

Yet the structural changes advancing beneath the drawdown are laying the groundwork for the next cycle: the entrenchment of the yield-bearing altcoin ETF category (eight straight weeks of XRP inflows), the resilient growth of RWA ($31.7B) and stablecoins ($314B), the institutionalization of staking (the SEC/CFTC joint interpretation), and on-chain valuation metrics at historic bottoms. Long-term holders accumulating directly against record ETF outflows is a particularly notable signal — with the caveat that these indicators are lagging and their signals compressed in the ETF era.

Q3 ultimately hinges on two axes: **the June CPI (7/14) and whether the Fed hikes**, and **whether the CLARITY Act clears before the August recess**. Near term, positioning should brace for elevated volatility and further downside tests while maintaining selective exposure to infrastructure, yield-bearing, and institutionally adopted assets (RWA, stablecoin infrastructure, yield-ETF underlyings, fee-generating protocols). On security, bridge-verifier configuration and operational security should now rank as first-order protocol due-diligence criteria.
