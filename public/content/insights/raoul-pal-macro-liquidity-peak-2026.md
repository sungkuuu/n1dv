This massive plunge of approximately 46% and the tedious correction phase spanning from late 2025 to early 2026 have caused severe confusion among market participants who believed in the traditional 4-year cycle theory. According to the existing model, the second half of 2025 was supposed to be the period when the supply shock from the reduction in Bitcoin block rewards maximized, forming the peak of an explosive rally. However, the actual market drew an entirely different trajectory, facing macroeconomic headwinds and fears of a liquidity crunch. This suggests that the cryptocurrency market is no longer an isolated ecosystem driven solely by internal protocol rules (halvings).

This report thoroughly verifies the four core pillars Raoul Pal presents as the basis for his 2026 peak theory—the '5.4-year macro debt cycle,' the 'Debt Wall and $8 trillion liquidity injection concentrated in 2026,' the 'ISM Manufacturing PMI turnaround,' and the 'mathematical inevitability of currency debasement due to structural demographic changes'—based on rigorous macroeconomic data and empirical indicators. ## 1. The End of the Bitcoin Halving Cycle and the Advent of the '5.4-Year Macro Debt Cycle'

The halving event, which cuts the Bitcoin network's block mining reward in half, acted as an absolute supply shock driver propelling price increases in the early Bitcoin ecosystem. However, as the total market capitalization of the global cryptocurrency market expanded to a multi-trillion-dollar scale, and assets from traditional finance flooded in via massive institutional capital from Wall Street and spot ETFs, the practical mathematical impact of the halving on the overall market has been diluted exponentially. The pricing power of Bitcoin has now completely shifted from the reduction of miners' selling pressure to whether global macro liquidity expands or contracts.

### The Everything Code and the Dominance of Liquidity

Raoul Pal explains this phenomenon through his unique macroeconomic framework called 'The Everything Code'. This framework is a theory that comprehensively synthesizes the trilemma of global demographic shifts, accumulated sovereign and corporate debt, and the policy liquidity inevitably required to roll over that debt. According to this theory, Bitcoin's price trajectory strictly tracks the expansion waves of global M2 (broad money) liquidity, and the cryptocurrency asset class functions as the best-performing long-duration asset amidst continuous fiat currency debasement.

### Michael Howell's 65-Month (5.4-Year) Liquidity Sine Wave Model

The 5.4-year cycle theory argued by Raoul Pal is strongly supported by the empirical research of Michael Howell, a leading authority on global liquidity analysis. According to Howell's modeling, the global liquidity created by central banks and private financial systems worldwide traces a massive sine wave trajectory, repeating expansion and contraction on a cycle of approximately 65 months (5.4 years).

Applying this framework to the current point in time, the current liquidity cycle is in its 'Late-cycle' phase, entering its 34th month as of early 2026. Under past natural economic environments, this cycle should have peaked around late 2025 and transitioned into a contraction phase. However, to artificially disperse the maturity dates of the massive scale of sovereign and private debt issued by governments during the COVID-19 pandemic and prevent systemic collapse, policymakers executed 'Policy Engineering' to significantly extend the timeframe of liquidity provision.

Raoul Pal analyzes that due to this artificial extension of the cycle, the log trend channels formed by asset markets have stretched further along the time axis, resulting in the natural deferral of the ultimate liquidity explosion and the timing of the asset market peak from 2025 to Q2 2026. Consequently, the 46% price drop Bitcoin experienced as it reverted from the $120,000 level to the $60,000 level from late 2025 to early 2026 does not signify the complete end of the cycle or an entry into a recession. Rather, their core logic is that this should be interpreted as a mid-cycle consolidation and liquidity reallocation process preparing for the Final Act—the ultimate and most explosive upward leg of the 5.4-year macro debt cycle.

## 2. The Dynamics of the 2026 'Debt Wall' and the $8 Trillion Macro Liquidity Injection

The most concrete and realistic trigger supporting Raoul Pal's 2026 peak theory is the unprecedented scale of 'Debt Maturity' schedules concentrated specifically in the year 2026. To prevent national economies from falling into default, governments must continuously issue new debt (Rollover) to pay the principal and interest on existing debt, and this massive rollover process inevitably injects astronomical amounts of new liquidity into the financial system.

### Verification of the US's Severe Debt Maturity Structure and Interest Burden

A close analysis of macroeconomic data confirms that Raoul Pal's argument is not mere speculation but is backed by clear statistical evidence. As of early March 2026, the US Gross National Debt has reached an astronomical scale of $38.86 trillion. Out of this, the Marketable Debt Held by the Public, excluding federal agencies, amounts to approximately $31.27 trillion alone.

The core problem threatening the system's survival here is the maturity structure of this debt. During the short 12-month period from April 1, 2025, to the end of March 2026, a situation arose where $9.3 trillion in treasury bonds—equivalent to about one-third of the total debt held by the public—reached maturity. According to a fiscal analysis report by the global accounting and consulting firm Deloitte, approximately $9.2 trillion in debt had already matured by the end of 2025, and subsequently, an additional $9 trillion in marketable debt is waiting, forming another Debt Wall throughout the year 2026.

This extreme concentration of maturities is no coincidence. During the COVID-19 pandemic, the US Treasury Department adopted an anomalous financing method to raise urgent funds by reducing the issuance of high-interest long-term bonds and drastically increasing the issuance of short-term T-bills. As of February 2026, short-term bills with maturities between 4 and 52 weeks (1 year or less) account for approximately 21.83% ($6.81 trillion) of the total US marketable debt. Financing focused on short-term bills created a temporary optical illusion of lowering the government's initial interest payment burden, but it has returned as a boomerang of extreme maturity pressure, requiring bonds to be newly rolled over continuously throughout the year.

### The Distribution of $8 Trillion in Liquidity and the Inevitability of Debt Monetization

Raoul Pal estimates that over the next 12 months (from 2026 to early 2027), approximately $8 trillion in cash liquidity must be newly printed and injected into the market to prevent the collapse of the global financial system, including the US. This is because massive debts previously issued at cheap zero-interest-rate levels (about 1-2%) must now be rolled over in a much higher interest rate environment, averaging over 3.355% as of February 2026. The exponential surge in interest costs due to rising interest rates is causing massive, uncontrollable fiscal deficits in the US federal budget, with the US federal deficit for 2026 alone projected to reach approximately $1.9 trillion.

To surmount this massive debt wall, the Fed and the US Treasury inevitably have no choice but to execute 'Debt Monetization'. The core shift Raoul Pal and Michael Howell note here is a fundamental policy paradigm shift from the traditional 'Fed QE (Quantitative Easing)' that dominated after the financial crisis to 'Treasury QE'.

Instead of the Fed stepping in directly to expand its balance sheet, the US Treasury is mechanically reducing the duration of assets held by the private sector and amplifying liquidity within the system by abnormally increasing the issuance of short-term T-bills, which are treated virtually identical to cash in the market. Furthermore, an interesting phenomenon in this process is that cryptocurrency-based stablecoin issuers like Tether are buying up T-bills en masse, emerging as a powerful new source of demand and a transmission channel for US dollar liquidity.

Raoul Pal highly praises this stablecoin structure as a "fractional-reserve-based Eurodollar market broken down and accessible at the retail level." This new infrastructure provides individuals worldwide with access to dollar liquidity, serving as a massive capital pipeline where the enormous debt issued by the Treasury passes through stablecoins and ultimately flows into the cryptocurrency asset market. Raoul Pal analyzes that a significant portion of the aforementioned $8 trillion in liquidity will flow into the cryptocurrency market through this transmission channel, driving the price explosion in 2026.

## 3. Structural Demographic Changes and the Mathematical Inevitability of Currency Debasement

The fundamental element that serves as the firmest foundation in Raoul Pal's 5.4-year macro-economic cycle model is not short-term government policy changes or market whims, but the irreversible 'demographic structural changes' that can absolutely never be reversed. The ongoing decline in global labor participation rates and the extreme slowdown in population growth rates signify an absolute decrease in the core working population that generates real wealth and pays taxes within the economic system. This inevitably causes a structural slowdown in a nation's potential GDP growth rate.

Looking mathematically at the 'Debt-to-GDP Ratio,' the most important indicator of economic health, the problem becomes even more severe. Due to population decline, the GDP growth rate (the denominator) slows sharply, while the national debt (the numerator) explodes due to increasing social security costs from aging populations and massive interest costs on national bonds. Under a mathematical formula where the denominator shrinks and the numerator grows, a continuous rise in the Debt-to-GDP ratio arrives at an inescapable conclusion.

The realistic options available to a sovereign nation trapped in this inescapable Debt Trap are extreme tax hikes, declaring sovereign default, or 'Currency Debasement'—alleviating the debt repayment burden by increasing the money supply and depreciating the value of fiat currency.

To avoid the first two options, which face massive political resistance, sovereign nations throughout history have always chosen the third method, currency debasement, in the face of crisis. It is exactly at this point that Raoul Pal clearly identifies the intrinsic value of Bitcoin. Amidst the relentless, exponential increase in global liquidity, the only assets capable of preserving purchasing power are Hard Money with a finite supply, such as Bitcoin and gold. He argues that Bitcoin, as the most immediate and excellent haven reacting to liquidity expansion, will experience a massive capital influx in 2026.

## 5. Verification of Fatal Logical Flaws and Macro Contradictions in Raoul Pal's Arguments

Raoul Pal's Q2 2026 peak theory provides a very interesting framework from a macro perspective. However, when cross-verifying the data and actual macroeconomic mechanisms, several grave logical flaws and contradictions are found in the core rationales supporting his claims.

### 1) Historical and Mathematical Contradictions of the 5.4-Year Cycle Theory (Curve-Fitting Past Data)

Raoul Pal claims that the past three cryptocurrency bull markets perfectly coincided with a '5.4-year (65-month) cycle' rather than the 4-year halving. However, this borders on a blatant mathematical error. Bitcoin's past major peaks formed in late 2013, late 2017, and late 2021, which are spaced at clear intervals of exactly 4 years (48 months). Forcing past market peaks that occurred at 4-year intervals into a 5.4-year macro liquidity cycle frame can be interpreted as typical Curve-fitting—a post-hoc confirmation bias to defend the bear market that fell short of expectations in late 2025 and justify the 2026 peak theory.

### 2) Exaggeration and Conceptual Errors of the '$8 Trillion Liquidity Injection'

Raoul Pal mechanically calculates that the government will print and inject about $8 trillion in cash over the next 12 months to roll over the massive debt maturing in 2026. It is a statistical fact that about $9 trillion of US marketable treasury bonds will mature in 2026. However, the act of extending the maturity of existing debt (Rollover) itself does not mean that $8 trillion in 'Net New Liquidity' is newly released into the market. The Treasury simply issues new debt to repay the matured debt to existing bondholders. The pure money practically newly created in the market is limited to the level of the US annual fiscal deficit (approx. $1.9 trillion). Interpreting the entire rollover volume as a massive $8 trillion liquidity explosion flowing into risk assets is a macroeconomic leap in logic.

### 3) The Paradox of Rising ISM PMI and Liquidity Crunches

He argues that if the US ISM Manufacturing PMI exceeds 50 and enters an expansion phase, Bitcoin absorbs liquidity and rises explosively. However, when the PMI representing the real economy showed a strong expansion trend at 52.4~52.6 in early 2026, a severe contradiction occurred instead. As the real economy proved too resilient and inflation pressures (Prices Paid Index at 59.0) strengthened, the Fed maintained its benchmark interest rate at the 3.50%~3.75% level at the March 2026 FOMC meeting, delaying rate cuts. His model overlooks the realistic paradox where a real economy boom (rising PMI) instead induces Fed tightening (prolonged high interest rates), which sustains a strong dollar phenomenon and suppresses the liquidity of high-beta assets like cryptocurrencies.

### 4) Conflicting Opinions with the Core Source (Michael Howell's Warning)

Raoul Pal relies heavily on the 65-month cycle model of Michael Howell, a global liquidity expert, for his 'Everything Code' theory. Yet, Michael Howell himself is pouring out pessimistic warnings about the 2026 market, completely opposite to Pal. Howell points out that as the real economy recovers in 2026 and massive capital is invested in building large-scale AI data centers, a 'Liquidity Tug-of-War' phenomenon will intensify, where speculative liquidity that should remain in financial markets (crypto and stocks) is sucked out into the real production sector. This implies that 2026 could be a very turbulent year for risk assets, rather than an unconditional explosion.

## 6. Conclusion: Comprehensive Probability Assessment of the 2026 Peak Theory

The long-term macro directional thesis pointed out by Raoul Pal—'structural population decline → national economic slowdown → national debt increase → currency debasement'—is macroeconomically valid in itself, making it highly probable that Bitcoin will trend upward in the long run. It is also difficult to deny that the macro pressure formed by massive treasury bond maturities ultimately provides a structural tailwind favorable to Bitcoin, which exists outside the fiat currency system.

However, when comprehensively weighing the logical contradictions verified above and the conflicting elements in the macro data, the probability of Raoul Pal's specific scenario—that "$8 trillion in liquidity will pour in during Q2 (or late) 2026, marking the massive peak of the cycle"—materializing exactly as stated is downgraded to below 30% to 40%.

Currently, the Fed is extremely controlling liquidity supply due to concerns about reigniting inflation, and the mechanical rollover of debt following treasury maturities does not translate directly into a net new capital expansion into the cryptocurrency market. Furthermore, the 'Liquidity Tug-of-War' triggered by the expansion of the real economy and the absorption of capital into the AI industry is highly likely to act as a very strong brake limiting Bitcoin rallies.

Therefore, rather than uncritically accepting Pal's theory that a blind, massive liquidity rally will arrive in 2026, market participants must continuously monitor the actual pace of the Fed's rate cuts, the Treasury's debt rollover methods, and the aspects of the capital scramble between the real economy and financial markets. Rather than relying on fixed time axes like the outdated 4-year cycle theory or the 5.4-year cycle theory, it is a time that requires a perspective of orchestrating exit strategies more defensively and flexibly according to changes in the macro environment.
